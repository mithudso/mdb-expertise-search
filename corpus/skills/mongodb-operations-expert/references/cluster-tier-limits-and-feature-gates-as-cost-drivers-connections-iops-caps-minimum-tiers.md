# Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers)

## Overview

On MongoDB Atlas, the cluster tier sets hard ceilings and unlocks features, so the first limit you hit, not CPU or RAM alone, can force an upgrade. Per-node connection limits run 500 (Free, Flex), 1,500 (M10), 3,000 (M20, M30) and 6,000 (M40), plus a separate new-connection rate cap. M10 and M20 use burstable CPU and throttle when credits run out. Storage IOPS depends on tier, disk size, hardware generation (Gen1 16k, Gen2 80k on AWS) and the cloud's own instance caps. Feature gates start at M10 (backup restore, private endpoints, auditing, Online Archive, Search Nodes) and M30 (sharding, Global Writes). Flex stops at 500 ops/s and Free at 100. Atlas Infinite, in public preview, fixes IOPS per tier. Figures are volatile: check the live tables.

## Core Concepts

### Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers)

- Atlas sets the maximum concurrent incoming connections by cluster tier, storage class and cloud. General-class per-node limits: Free and Flex 500; M10 1,500; M20 3,000; M30 3,000. When a node reaches its limit, it opens no new connections until open connections drop below the limit. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-1][^c1-15][^c1-4][^c1-6][^c1-19]
- Tentative: Connection limits apply per node. In a sharded cluster they apply per mongos router, and the router count equals the number of replica set nodes across all shards. Atlas reserves 10 connections per node for its own services. A three-node M10 with 1,500 per node can serve 2,980 connections from its two secondaries when the read preference is secondary. verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-1]
- Storage class and cloud change the connection ceiling. On AWS, Low-CPU M40 allows 4,000 connections per node against 6,000 for General M40; on Azure and Google Cloud, Low-CPU M40 allows 6,000. Low-CPU tiers carry half the vCPUs of the General tier of the same size and may carry fewer maximum connections. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-1][^c1-3]
- AWS us-east-1 General-class ladder, base price per hour (default storage included) and per-node connection ceiling: M10 $0.08, 1,500; M20 $0.20, 3,000; M30 $0.54, 3,000; M40 $1.04, 6,000; M50 $2.00, 16,000; M60 $3.95, 32,000; M80 $7.30, 96,000. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-5][^c1-1]
- Tentative: Atlas also caps new connections per second per node. General class: M10 15; M20 15; M30 24; M40 85; M50 170; M60 320; M80 1,000; M140 1,360; M200 1,450; M300 1,600. At the cap, Atlas queues new attempts and drops some connections if overload persists. The cap is always on for M10 and M20. For M30 and larger it is on only with MongoDB 9.0 or later and off on earlier versions. By arithmetic, admitting 500 new connections at 15 per second takes about 33 seconds. verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-1]
- Tentative: Atlas states that cluster connection limits are enforced for AWS only when clients connect over private link, on MongoDB 8.1 and 8.1.0 or later, 8.0 and 8.0.10 or later, and 7.0 and 7.0.22 or later. The page does not say how limits apply on other AWS connection paths. verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-1]
- Atlas auto-scaling responds to memory and compute use, not to connection counts. A connection limit alone does not raise the tier. The documented scale-up criteria are CPU, memory, and queued or rejected operations. The documented ways past a connection limit: tune the application, enable connection pooling, enable auto-scaling for the load the connections cause, or scale the tier permanently. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-19[^c1-1][^c1-16]
- M10 and M20 run on burstable-performance infrastructure on AWS, Azure and Google Cloud. Atlas warns that cloud providers cap CPU after burst periods, so heavy load causes throttling. MongoDB describes M10 and M20 as shared-vCPU tiers, and M30 as the first tier with dedicated vCPUs and the recommended tier for production. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-2][^c1-5][^c1-7]
- On AWS T-family burstable instances, the instance earns CPU credits at a fixed rate and spends them above a baseline utilization. Baseline per vCPU is 20% for t3.small and t3.medium, 30% for t3.large, and 40% for t3.xlarge and t3.2xlarge. In standard mode, an instance with no accrued credits drops gradually to baseline and cannot burst until it accrues credits. Atlas puts baseline at about 20% to 50% for burstable types and does not show it in Atlas metrics.[^c1-9][^c1-8][^c1-16]
- Tentative: The pricing tables list M10 as 2 vCPUs on AWS, 1 on Azure and 0.5 on Google Cloud. M20 is 2, 1 and 1. M30 is 2 on all three. Google Cloud lists M10 with 1.7 GB RAM and M20 with 3.75 GB, against 2 GB and 4 GB on AWS and Azure. The same tier name does not mean the same hardware across clouds. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-5]
- Flex clusters cap read and write operations at 500 per second, allow 500 connections and 5 GB of data, and allow 100 databases and 500 collections. Above 500 ops per second Flex enforces a hard limit with a sub-second cooldown and queued operations, not a higher price tier. Flex bills usage tiers from $0.011 per hour (up to 100 ops per second, $8 per month) to $0.0411 per hour (400 to 500 ops per second, $30 per month). verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-4][^c1-24][^c1-5]
- Free clusters allow 100 operations per second, 500 connections and 0.5 GB (512 MB) of data. They throttle after 10 GB in and 10 GB out per rolling seven days. Atlas pauses a Free cluster after 30 days with zero connections. Free clusters have no backups, network peering, private endpoints or database auditing. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-6][^c1-5][^c1-2]
- Atlas stopped creating M2, M5 and Serverless instances in February 2025. On January 22, 2026 it ended support, migrated all M2 and M5 clusters to Flex, and moved Serverless instances to Free, Flex or Dedicated clusters according to usage. Migrated M2 and M5 customers moved from a flat monthly price to ops-per-second pricing. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-22][^c1-24]
- Leaving Flex for the first Dedicated tier roughly doubles the monthly floor. Flex tops out at $30 per month at 500 ops per second. M10 on AWS us-east-1 is $0.08 per hour, which the pricing page estimates at $57 per month. M10 also raises the connection ceiling from 500 to 1,500 per node. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-5][^c1-24][^c1-1]
- Tier moves toward Flex are blocked. Atlas lets you scale a Free cluster to Flex or Dedicated at any time. It does not let you scale an M10+ Dedicated cluster down to Free or Flex, and you cannot change a cluster's database edition (Atlas Core or Atlas Infinite) after creation. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-25][^c1-2]
- Atlas allows sharded clusters only on M30 or larger. Converting a replica set to a sharded cluster cannot be reversed. M10 and M20 support replica sets only.[^c1-25][^c1-2][^c1-17]
- Sharded clusters add config servers, which run on M30 and are billed separately from the data-bearing servers. The Atlas limits page lists the config server tier as M30 for both minimum and maximum. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-2][^c1-17][^c1-1]
- Global Writes requires an M30 or larger sharded cluster, or a replica set scaled to at least M30. You cannot disable Global Writes after you enable it. While Global Writes is on, Global Clusters do not support dedicated Search Nodes. Flex clusters do not support Global Clusters.[^c1-22][^c1-25][^c1-26]
- Dedicated clusters (M10 and larger) unlock features that Free and Flex lack: Cloud Backup with continuous backup and point-in-time restore (Flex has one automatic daily snapshot, no on-demand snapshots and no point-in-time restore; Free has no backups), private endpoints, network peering, database auditing, encryption at rest with customer-managed keys, the Performance Advisor, the Real-Time Performance Panel, cross-region deployment, and dedicated analytics or search nodes. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-2][^c1-4][^c1-6][^c1-17]
- Online Archive is available only on M10 and larger clusters. Flex clusters do not support it. Atlas charges a fee for each archive partition a query reads, and data transferred to the federated infrastructure incurs data transfer cost.[^c1-13][^c1-14]
- MongoDB Search and Vector Search run on Free, Flex and M10+ clusters with the mongot process on the same node as mongod. Dedicated Search Nodes need an M10+ cluster. Atlas enforces a minimum of two Search Nodes (maximum 32 per region per shard) and bills each node hourly. Google Cloud supports Search Nodes in all regions, AWS and Azure in a subset. MongoDB notes that sharing a node can cost less than separate nodes but may cause resource contention, so it recommends separate Search Nodes for production. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-20][^c1-1][^c1-4][^c1-24]
- Gen1 dedicated clusters scale storage and standard IOPS together and top out at 16k standard IOPS on AWS and 100k on Google Cloud. Gen2 clusters (M30 and larger, AWS and Google Cloud only, no multi-cloud) scale and bill storage and IOPS independently and allow up to 80k IOPS on AWS and 160k on Google Cloud. Gen2 supports reactive auto-scaling only. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-2][^c1-3][^c1-23]
- M30 and larger clusters start at 3,000 standard IOPS. This matches the 3,000 IOPS baseline that an EBS gp3 volume includes in its storage price. On Gen2 the baseline stays 3,000 below 1,000 GB of disk and then rises 3 IOPS per GB (2,000 GB gives 6,000; 4,000 GB gives 12,000). (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-3][^c1-10]
- Atlas's Gen2 ceiling on AWS (80k IOPS) equals the current EBS gp3 maximum (80,000 IOPS, 2,000 MiB/s), and its Gen1 ceiling (16k) equals the gp2 maximum (16,000 IOPS, reached at 5,334 GiB). Atlas documents standard IOPS only as General Purpose SSD, so the volume type behind Gen2 is an inference. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-10][^c1-2][^c1-3]
- On AWS, M30 and larger clusters can buy Provisioned IOPS (Gen1) or High Performance IOPS (Gen2). These use Provisioned IOPS SSD volumes, designed to deliver the provisioned rate 99.9% of the time, against 99% for the General Purpose SSD behind standard IOPS. Custom storage speed raises cost. For Gen1 the allowed range depends on disk size; for Gen2 it does not. Gen1 NVMe clusters cannot customize IOPS. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-3][^c1-23][^c1-10]
- Instance size caps disk IOPS regardless of what you provision. On AWS, EBS performance is bounded by the lower of the instance type's limit and the attached volumes' aggregate. Smaller sizes sustain their maximum for only 30 minutes at least once per 24 hours, then revert to baseline (per the AWS table, m5.large: baseline 3,600, maximum 18,750 IOPS). On Azure, VM size sets IOPS and throughput limits apart from disk limits, so a Standard_D2s_v3 cannot reach a P30 disk's 5,000 IOPS. Atlas adds that AWS-listed instance maximums can exceed what Atlas lets you configure. verified-as-of: 2026-09-16[^c1-11][^c1-18][^c1-21]
- Atlas enforces a disk-to-RAM ratio of 60:1 for M10 to M40 and 120:1 above M40, so data size can force a larger tier: 3 TB needs at least 32 GB of RAM (M50 or larger). The pricing table's storage maxima fit these ratios: M40 (16 GB RAM) up to 1 TB; M50 (32 GB RAM) up to 4 TB. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-3][^c1-5]
- Tentative: Extended storage lets single-region AWS Core clusters in General or Low-CPU class exceed default disk limits: M40 up to 4 TB; M50 and M60 up to 8 TB; M80 and larger up to 14 TB. Atlas warns it slows initial syncs and cross-project snapshot restores and can reduce high availability, and recommends sharding for long-term growth. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-3]
- Local NVMe tiers (M40 and larger; AWS and Azure only, not Google Cloud) have fixed storage per tier, for example 380 GB on AWS M40_NVMe and 760 GB on M50_NVMe. They auto-scale to the next tier when 90% of storage is used. Gen1 NVMe clusters must use standard IOPS. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-25][^c1-21][^c1-3]
- Tentative: Atlas recommends at most 5,000 combined collections and indexes on M10, 10,000 on M20 and M30, and 100,000 on M40 and larger. Above that, checkpoints take longer, operations slow, and operations can stall at the ulimit. Atlas suggests a larger tier to raise the limit. verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-1]
- On Azure, IOPS rise with disk size at no extra charge. Extra IOPS cost more, are available on M40 and larger in regions that support extended storage, and cannot exceed the tier's maximum. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-3][^c1-23]
- On Atlas Infinite (public preview) the cluster tier fixes storage IOPS and throughput, and they do not grow with data; changing performance means changing the tier. On Atlas Core, storage performance scales with disk capacity. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-2][^c1-3][^c1-21]
- Tentative: Atlas Infinite public-preview storage performance by tier (16 kB I/O reference values): M10 625 IOPS and 10 MBps; M20 1,250 and 20; M30 5,000 and 80; M40 9,375 and 150; M50 18,750 and 300; M60 37,500 and 600. Low-CPU: M40 5,000 and 80; M50 9,375 and 150; M60 18,750 and 300. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-21]
- Tentative: The pricing page lists Atlas Infinite as a public preview on M10+ dedicated clusters, starting at $0.09 per hour, with up to 128 TB per shard and 1M+ IOPS burst performance. verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-5]

- Tentative: The Intelligent Workload Management section appears on M10 and larger clusters, but Load Shedding takes effect only on M30+ replica sets running MongoDB 9.0 or later; on M10 and M20 the setting appears and does nothing. Adaptive capacity takes effect on M30 and larger. verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-17]

- Atlas derives the maximum number of concurrent incoming connections from the cluster tier and storage class (it sets net.maxIncomingConnections), and the limit applies per node, not per cluster.[^c1-29][^c1-39]
- Tentative: On sharded clusters the connection limit applies per mongos router, and Atlas runs one mongos per replica-set node across all shards. The docs' worked example, a three-node M10 with 1,500 connections per node and 10 reserved per node, gives a combined 2,980 when reads go to the two secondaries.[^c1-29]
- General-class maximum connections per node are M10 1,500, M20 3,000, M30 3,000 and M40 6,000, so M20 and M30 share one ceiling. verified-as-of: 2026-10-20[^c1-1][^c1-44][^c1-19]

- Atlas Infinite (public preview) supports only M10-M60 tiers and fixes storage IOPS and throughput per tier instead of scaling them with disk size, so changing IOPS means changing tier. - Atlas exposes the signals that show a tier limit biting: the 'Connections % of configured limit' alert and Connections chart, 'Disk read/write IOPS on Data Partition' alerts, the Disk IOPS graph compared with configured IOPS, IOWait in Normalized System CPU, and the CPU (Steal) % alert on AWS M10/M20.[^c1-15][^c1-38][^c1-7] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_

## Methodology

- Atlas documents these steps when a cluster nears its connection limit: fix code that opens connections and never closes them, enable driver connection pooling, enable cluster auto-scaling, and scale the tier. The 'Connections % of configured limit' alert warns before the limit. Free and Flex clusters can alert only on Connections, Logical Size, Network and Opscounter.[^c1-1][^c1-15][^c1-4] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Tentative: For M10 and M20, Atlas judges scale-up on Relative System CPU Utilization, which is Normalized System CPU divided by baseline CPU utilization, capped at 100%. On AWS it scales up when relative utilization stays above 90% for 20 minutes and CPU steal stays above 30% for 3 minutes, or when other listed conditions hold. Scale-down needs Normalized System CPU below 45% of baseline (about 9% absolute at a 20% baseline) over the last 10 minutes and 4 hours. verified-as-of: 2026-10-19[^c1-16] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Budget connections as maxPoolSize times application servers per mongod: with the default maxPoolSize of 100, four application servers can open up to 400 connections to each node. Add monitoring overhead: MongoDB staff say each Lambda instance needs at least three connections (one for the operation, one monitoring, one round-trip-time).[^c1-41][^c1-42] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_

## Practical Patterns

- MongoDB's guidance is to start lower, monitor, and scale up; use auto-scaling on Dedicated tiers; and treat M30 and larger as the production tier. The tier is the foundational cost driver, with storage, data transfer, backups and add-ons (Search Nodes, Stream Processing, support) on top. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-5][^c1-2] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Tentative: If you customize storage, Atlas bills the full custom size and does not credit the default storage. Example: an M10 raised from 10 GB to 50 GB bills for 50 GB. Changing class, for example General to Low-CPU, has the same effect. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-23] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- After a Gen1 cluster switches to Gen2, it may keep more IOPS than the Gen2 baseline included with its storage. Atlas recommends re-tuning storage and IOPS separately to cut cost. ## Anti-Patterns

- Moving from M20 to M30 raises no connection ceiling: both list 3,000 per node in the General class. The first step up is M40 at 6,000. On AWS us-east-1 the base price rises from $0.20 per hour (M20) to $0.54 (M30), then to $1.04 (M40). (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-1][^c1-5] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Buying IOPS beyond what the instance can move wastes money, because the instance cap applies whatever the disk provisions (AWS EBS-optimized limits; Azure VM limits). Check the instance's EBS or VM IOPS limit before raising provisioned IOPS.[^c1-11][^c1-18] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Running sustained production load on M10 or M20 risks CPU-credit throttling that looks like a node slowdown, not a connection or disk problem. Atlas documents the CPU steal alert for these tiers and recommends M30 and larger for production. (two sources)[^c1-2][^c1-7] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Moving from M20 to M30 adds no connection capacity (both 3,000 per node) while the AWS list price rises 2.7 times ($0.20 to $0.54 per hour); M30 to M40 doubles the cap to 6,000 for about 1.9 times the price ($1.04). This is arithmetic derived from the limits table and the price table. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-1][^c1-5] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Creating a MongoClient per request or Lambda invocation multiplies connection pools and causes connection storms that can pass for a capacity shortage. In one forum case an M30 at 95% of its 3,000 limit was upgraded to M40 and connections still peaked at 3-4.5K; the thread reached no confirmed resolution. (two sources)[^c1-39][^c1-42] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Running sustained load on M10 or M20 exhausts burstable CPU credits. A practitioner running M20 on Azure reports a B-series VM with a 40% CPU baseline and no CPU Steal metric or alert on Azure, which matches the Atlas doc that the alert covers only AWS burstable instances. (two sources)[^c1-19][^c1-7] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- Buying IOPS by enlarging the disk is costly: when you customize storage Atlas bills the full storage amount without deducting the tier's default storage, and on Gen1 storage and IOPS scale together so the bill reflects both. (two sources) verified-as-of: 2026-10-14[^c1-31][^c1-2] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_

## Troubleshooting

- Tentative: For immediate relief at the connection limit on M10 and larger clusters, Test Primary Failover steps down the primary and drops all its connections. - Tentative: A November 2020 MongoDB Community thread reports a Vercel/Lambda user hitting the Atlas connection limit when functions scaled up. - The System: CPU (Steal) % alert fires when usage exceeds the baseline CPU credit accumulation rate and the EC2 credit balance is exhausted. - Tentative: On MongoDB's feedback portal, users have asked since June 2022 for CPU-credit metrics on M10 and M20 (status Submitted, no staff reply visible). - AWS allows at most four modifications per EBS volume in a rolling 24-hour period and requires each to complete first. Atlas's fast disk pre-warming on M40 and larger non-NVMe AWS clusters consumes one of the four. verified-as-of: 2026-10-18[^c1-3][^c1-12][^c1-25] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- On M40 and larger AWS clusters without NVMe, Atlas can temporarily provision a higher-performance volume when it replaces or adds a node (fast disk pre-warming). - Tentative: On Azure clusters that support extended storage and IOPS, Atlas allows up to four disk IOPS or throughput changes in any 24-hour period. - Tentative: One practitioner running Atlas on Azure reports (2021 to 2022 observations) single small-document inserts taking up to 7 to 15 seconds, a problem that persisted after upgrading to M30 and that they attribute to Azure disk IOPS throttling. These are one customer's observations, partly relayed from support. verified-as-of: 2025-01-17[^c1-19] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
- When clients exceed the tier limit Atlas can refuse new connections with the error 'connection refused because too many open connections', and the 'Connections % of configured limit' alert fires. (two sources)[^c1-15][^c1-4] _(Cluster Tier Limits and Feature Gates as Cost Drivers (Connections, IOPS Caps, Minimum Tiers))_
